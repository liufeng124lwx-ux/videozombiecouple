## The trend, with your pet

The zombie love story is a short vertical AI video that spread in September and October 2026: one partner has turned, the other can't fire, they hold each other, and it cuts to a happy memory. Pet owners started making their own version almost at once, with the dog or cat as the one who turned.

It works because the question is the same: would you still hold on? With a pet the answer is never in doubt, and that is what makes it land.

## How the pet version is built

The film is <F k="video.seconds" /> seconds long, in four shots and an end title.

1. **Standoff.** A slow push-in from behind you toward your pet, who has turned, in cold blue light.
2. **The hand.** A close-up of your hand. The flashlight trembles and the beam slowly lowers.
3. **The embrace.** You set the light down and hold them.
4. **The memory.** A match cut to a sunlit field. Same pose, same framing, both of you well.
5. **End title.** A one-second card with a line, your names and a date, if you want them.

## Dogs, cats and other pets

Dogs and cats work best. Their faces are easy for the models to read, and their coats give the likeness something to hold on to: a tabby's stripes, a husky's mask, a spaniel's ears. Short-haired pets with clear markings come out most like themselves. Very fluffy pets and all-black coats are harder, because there is less detail to match, so pick a photo where the light shows their fur.

Other animals may work, but they are not what we tested. Rabbits and birds sometimes come out as a different animal of the same kind. You see the key frames before you pay, so you lose nothing by trying.

In the pet version your pet is always the one who turned and you are always the survivor. The zombie look on a pet is the same restrained idea as on people: cloudy eyes and patchy, dull fur, with no wounds and nothing frightening. The film is about not letting go, not about horror.

## Picking a scene

All three scenes work with pets.

- **Cabin → Wheat field.** The original: blue moonlight through broken boards, then a sunset field. The one in the examples above.
- **Rainy city → Chapel.** A rain-soaked street at night, then a small chapel with white flowers.
- **Lighthouse → Beach.** A ruined lighthouse at dusk, then a beach at golden hour. Lovely for dogs who loved the sea.

## A film for a pet you lost

Many people make this for a pet that has died. The film ends on the two of you together in warm light, which is why it can feel like a goodbye rather than a horror clip. If that is why you are here, use a photo where they look like themselves on a good day. Add their name and the years to the end title if you like.

## Getting a good result

Use a sharp photo of your pet looking at the camera, with the whole head visible and the coat in good light. Markings matter: the model keeps what it can see. A photo of you facing the camera keeps you recognisable in the memory frame.
