## What the AI zombie filter does

The AI zombie filter takes one photo of you and returns a single portrait of you as a zombie. It is not a sticker or a face mask laid over your picture. The image is generated again from your photo: your face shape, eyes, nose, mouth, hairstyle and clothes stay, and the zombie look is painted on top of them. The background becomes a ruined room in cold blue light, so the photo reads as a still from a film rather than a selfie with an effect.

Most people want two things from a zombie filter, and they pull in opposite directions. They want it to be unmistakably zombie, and they want their friends to recognise them at a glance. Push the look too far, with sunken eyes, hollow cheeks and rotting skin, and the result stops being you. Keep it too light and it looks like a bad night's sleep. The filter aims for the middle: a grey-green tint, dark shadows around cloudy white eyes, a web of dark veins at the temples and neck, a blank stare and dust in the hair. Your bone structure is never changed.

## Why it keeps your skin tone

Many zombie filters paint the same pale grey over every face. On darker skin that reads as lightening the person, not turning them into a zombie, and it makes the result look like someone else. This filter is told to keep your skin exactly as dark or as light as it is in your photo and to add the sickly tint on top. We tested this on people with very different skin tones, hair and head coverings before putting it live. Hijabs, braids, locs and glasses stay as they are in your photo.

## No blood, no gore

The zombie look here is the restrained one from the zombie love story trend: sad rather than scary. There is no blood, no wounds, no bite marks, no open mouth and no teeth. That keeps the photo shareable anywhere, keeps your face readable, and keeps the result closer to good film makeup than to a horror poster.

## Photos that work best

- **One person, facing the camera.** A head-and-shoulders photo works best. Group photos are refused, because the filter only turns one person.
- **Eyes visible.** Take off sunglasses and keep hair away from your eyes. The cloudy zombie eyes need your eyes to work with.
- **Good, even light.** Daylight from a window is ideal. Very dark photos or strong coloured light make the face harder to keep.
- **Sharp and recent.** A blurry or heavily filtered photo gives the AI less of your face to keep. An old photo of you as a child is refused: the filter is for adults only.

## What we check before we make anything

Before the zombie photo is made, we check your upload. On our own servers we look for exactly one face and estimate the person's age; because age estimates can be off by several years, we refuse photos where someone looks younger than <F k="age.minEstimated" />. Our moderation provider checks the photo for nudity or sexual content, and such photos are refused. Only a photo that passes is turned into a zombie portrait. A refused photo doesn't use up your free photo for the day.

You also confirm, before every upload, that the photo is of you or of an adult who agreed to it. Please don't upload photos of celebrities, strangers or anyone who hasn't said yes.

## Your photo and your privacy

We use your photo once, to make your zombie portrait, and delete both the upload and the result after <F k="photo.keepHours" /> hours. Download the result before then. Face features used for the age check exist only in memory while the check runs and are never stored, sold or shared. We never use your photos to train AI. The finished photo carries a small mark in the corner, and the file itself says that it was generated with AI, as the law in more and more places expects.
